# 💼 Module 15 — LinkedIn Marketing

> 🟠 Stage 3 · Social, Email & Ads · ⏱️ ~4 hours

Mitti's corporate gifting posts said, "Elevate your corporate gifting experience." Nobody replied. Then Meera wrote: *"We quoted 300 mugs last week and lost the order. The HR manager told me why: our invoice had no purchase-order field. Fixed today. What else slows down small vendors?"* Forty-one comments. Three enquiries.

Professionals respond to specific experience. They scroll past slogans.

> 🔑 **In short:** LinkedIn marketing works through specific lessons from real work, thoughtful comments, a short list of relevant companies and respectful outreach. Judge it by qualified conversations and won customers, not impressions.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
- Decide between personal profile and company page.
- Write posts from real work.
- Build a small account list and start respectful conversations.
- Understand LinkedIn Ads and when their cost makes sense.

## 1. When LinkedIn Fits

| Fit | Question |
|---|---|
| Buyer has a work role | Can you name the role and company type? |
| Value is high enough | Will one customer repay slow, costly work? |
| Expertise matters | Do you have real cases or opinions? |
| Hiring/partnership | Do you need talent or partners? |

Selling ₹400 mugs one at a time? Probably not. Selling 300 at a time to HR teams? Worth testing.

## 2. People First, Page Second

| Asset | Job |
|---|---|
| Founder/expert profile | trust, conversation |
| Company page | credibility, hiring, ads, product information |
| Newsletter/article | depth |
| Document/carousel | steps and frameworks |
| Event/live | demonstration and questions |
| Groups | niche, if active |

People usually prefer to talk to people. A company page still matters when a buyer checks whether you're real.

### Profile as a landing page

❌ **Weak headline**
```text
Founder | Visionary | Disruptor | Dreamer
```

✅ **Strong headline**
```text
I help HR teams send handmade gifts people keep | Founder, Mitti Studio
```

- Photo: clear, recent.
- Banner: one sentence and proof.
- About: who, problem, approach, evidence, next step.
- Featured: gifting page, sample, case.
- Experience: outcomes and role, with permission.

### Company page checklist

- [ ] Plain one-line description
- [ ] Website, industry, size, location
- [ ] Button to the right page
- [ ] Two administrators
- [ ] Three recent useful posts

## 3. Post From Work

Keep a **work log**. Each day note one mistake, question, choice, number or disagreement.

| Type | Mitti example |
|---|---|
| Lesson | why we lost the 300-mug order |
| Breakdown | true cost of a custom gift |
| Checklist | 9 questions before ordering gifts |
| Contrarian | "Don't put a big logo on it" |
| Process | how a sample is approved |
| Proof | before/after packaging test |
| Question | what makes vendors easy to work with? |

### Structure

```text
Line 1-2: specific tension or result
Context: just enough
What happened / steps
What we learned, including limits
One question or next step
```

❌ **Weak**
```text
I am humbled and thrilled to announce that we are excited to leverage
synergies in the gifting space. Agree?
```

✅ **Strong**
```text
"Can you deliver 200 by the 12th?" We said yes too fast.

Two glazes failed. We delivered 140 and paid for a courier for the rest.

Now every bulk quote has three dates: sample, firing buffer, dispatch.
It looks less impressive, and HR managers thank us for it.

If you order custom gifts, ask vendors: "What's your failure buffer?"
```

Avoid inventing emotional stories. People in your field notice, and colleagues will know.

### Document posts

Turn a useful checklist into 6–10 readable pages. One idea per page, large text, a final page with a next step. People save and forward checklists to teammates.

## 4. Comments Are Content

Ten thoughtful comments a week on posts your buyers read can beat five posts of your own.

```text
Add: a detail, counterexample, number or useful question.
Don't: "Great post!", pitch, or copy AI text into every thread.
```

## 5. Outreach Without Being "That Person"

### Build a list of 30

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Company | 200–800 employees, Kolkata |
| Trigger | hiring many people, anniversary, new office |
| Person | HR/people operations, procurement |
| Relevance | onboarding gifts |
| Source | public role, event, mutual contact |
| Next step/date | comment, then note |

Use information lawfully and within platform rules. Automated scraping and mass-connection tools can break terms and damage accounts.

### Connection note

❌ **Weak**
```text
Hi, I want to connect and explore synergies. Can we get on a call?
```

✅ **Strong**
```text
Hi {{name}}, your post about first-week onboarding was useful, especially
the desk-kit idea. We make ceramic desk sets in Kolkata, and I'm
collecting what HR teams need from small vendors. Happy to connect.
```

### Message ladder

1. Useful connection, no pitch.
2. Answer or resource tied to their situation.
3. A specific, easy question.
4. Offer a sample or short call.
5. Stop after a reasonable follow-up.

```text
You mentioned 60 joiners in January. I made a one-page checklist of lead
times and invoice details HR teams asked us for. Want it? No call needed.
```

## 6. Sell to a Group, Not One Person

| Person | Wants |
|---|---|
| HR manager | employees enjoy it, no drama |
| Procurement | price, terms, invoice, vendor papers |
| Finance | tax and approvals |
| Brand team | logo quality |
| Founder | impression |

Prepare one short asset for each: sample, price sheet, GST details, artwork guide, case story.

## 7. Measure

| Level | Metric |
|---|---|
| Right audience | share of viewers/commenters in target roles |
| Conversation | useful comments, replies |
| Intent | profile and page visits, checklist requests |
| Pipeline | qualified meetings, samples, quotes, won value |

```text
Pipeline per hour = qualified pipeline value / hours spent
```

A post with 900 views and two HR managers asking for samples beats one with 60,000 views from students, if you sell corporate gifts.

## 8. LinkedIn Ads

LinkedIn can target by professional attributes, and usually costs more per click than many social networks. Third-party sources report a low daily minimum, but a practical test needs more. Confirm minimums in Campaign Manager.

| Objective | Asset |
|---|---|
| Awareness in a role | document, video |
| Consideration | case, checklist, employee post promoted with permission |
| Leads | lead-gen form or page |
| Retargeting | proof for visitors, where permitted |

### Do the maths first

Mitti's average corporate order: 150 sets × ₹1,300 = ₹1,95,000. Contribution 30% = ₹58,500. Suppose:

```text
Cost per lead            ₹2,500
Lead -> qualified        40%
Qualified -> won         15%
Cost per won order = 2500 / (0.40 x 0.15) = ₹41,667
```

₹41,667 is below ₹58,500, leaving ₹16,833 before sales time and overheads. At a 10% win rate, cost becomes ₹62,500 and it loses money. The rate you can't control yet decides the result, so test small.

### Lead form or landing page?

| Lead form | Landing page |
|---|---|
| easy, prefilled | more explanation |
| more volume | often clearer intent |
| needs fast follow-up | needs good mobile page |

Ask one qualifying question. Connect the form to the CRM (Module 23).

> 👔 **45 minutes a day for six weeks:** 10 min comments, 15 min writing, 10 min list, 10 min replies.

> 💻 **Tech:** pass lead ID, campaign and consent to CRM; import qualified and won stages if available; deduplicate by lead ID; restrict who sees contact data.

## 🛠️ Practical Lab: Build a LinkedIn Pipeline Without Spam

**You will finish with:** a clear profile, company Page, 30 accounts and five useful conversations. Time: 6 hours plus six weeks.

### Register

| Asset | Open |
|---|---|
| Profile | [linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/) |
| Company Page | [linkedin.com/company/setup/new](https://www.linkedin.com/company/setup/new/) |
| Ads | [LinkedIn Campaign Manager](https://www.linkedin.com/campaignmanager/) |
| Public ads | [LinkedIn Ad Library](https://www.linkedin.com/ad-library/) |

### Profile, step by step

1. Use your real name and recent photo.
2. Headline: `I help {{role}} {{outcome}} | {{real role}}`.
3. Banner: one problem and proof.
4. About: who, work, evidence, limits, contact.
5. Featured: useful checklist and one honest case study.
6. Experience: your role, dates and what you did.
7. Turn on two-step verification and review visibility.

### Company Page, step by step

1. Add the role to your real profile. You need to be an authorized representative.
2. LinkedIn → **For Business → Create a Company Page** → Company.
3. Enter name, public URL, website, industry, size, logo, tagline; confirm authorization.
4. Add About, location, specialties, action button and admins.
5. Publish three useful posts before inviting relevant connections.
6. Add your profile as admin and one backup admin.

### Find 30 accounts

1. Search companies by location, size and need. Use public information.
2. Score need, fit, trigger, accessible person and possible value 0–2.
3. Save only business information you need: company, reason, source, role, next step.
4. Follow ten and comment usefully on five over two weeks.
5. Send five personal notes. Don't upload/scrape contacts or use mass automation.

### A six-week routine

| Day | 25–45 minutes |
|---|---|
| Mon | publish one work-log lesson |
| Tue | ten thoughtful comments |
| Wed | improve one asset for a buyer |
| Thu | five relevant account checks, one message |
| Fri | reply, log conversations, prepare follow-up |

Use this follow-up: answer, useful resource, one question, stop.

### Publish a document post

1. Make a 6–10 page checklist in Canva or Slides, export PDF.
2. Upload as document, use a clear title and add a short introduction.
3. Check every page on mobile.
4. Offer the editable version only to people who ask; don't make comments a fake access gate.

### Small ad test

1. Confirm won-customer economics first.
2. Campaign Manager → Create → Lead generation or Website conversions.
3. Name by `YYYY-MM_objective_audience_offer`.
4. Location and language first; add job/industry attributes without making it tiny.
5. Review estimated audience, expansion and network settings.
6. Set daily/lifetime budget, loss limit and end date; confirm current minimum.
7. Build one clear document or single-image creative.
8. Lead form: only necessary fields, privacy notice and one qualifier.
9. Connect and test CRM export, then launch.
10. Judge qualified conversations and won value, not form fills.

### If it goes wrong

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| No replies | better company fit and more useful opening |
| Page can't be created | profile age/completeness, role and duplicate name |
| Lead is student/vendor | better targeting/qualifier; suppress later |
| Connection warning | stop activity; avoid automation and irrelevant requests |

### Proof to save

- [ ] Profile/Page before-after
- [ ] 30 scored accounts, private data protected
- [ ] 20 useful comments, five messages
- [ ] Conversation and pipeline outcomes, even if zero

## ❓ Common Questions

### Is it better to post from a LinkedIn profile or a company page?

Use a person's profile for conversation and trust, and a company page for credibility, hiring and ads. Small businesses usually get more from a founder or expert posting real experience.

### What should I post on LinkedIn?

Keep a work log of mistakes, questions, choices and results, then post one specific lesson with its limits. Checklists and process breakdowns also work well because colleagues save and share them.

### When are LinkedIn Ads worth it?

When one customer is valuable enough to cover expensive leads. At ₹2,500 per lead, 40% qualified and 15% of those won, each customer costs about ₹41,667. Compare that with contribution before starting.

## 🔑 Key Takeaways

- Real work beats slogans.
- Comments and conversations are part of the plan.
- B2B buying involves several people.
- Calculate ad economics from won deals.
