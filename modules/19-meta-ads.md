# 📣 Module 19 — Meta Ads (Facebook & Instagram)

> 🟠 Stage 3 · Social, Email & Ads · ⏱️ ~6 hours

Nobody opens Instagram to see your catalogue. You are interrupting a friend's photo. A good paid-social ad earns that interruption by showing a problem, a result or a story worth noticing.

> 🔑 **In short:** Meta Ads show Facebook and Instagram users an offer they weren't searching for, so creative does much of the work. Test different customer problems and proof, optimize for a real outcome, and scale only while the extra orders stay profitable.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

- Build a paid-social campaign around one outcome.
- Plan meaningfully different creatives.
- Test and scale with rules.
- Diagnose results without trusting one dashboard.

---

## 1. Three Levels

| Level | Main choice |
|---|---|
| Campaign | objective and budget approach |
| Ad set | audience, location, schedule, conversion location |
| Ad | creative, copy, destination |

Choose the real outcome. If you optimize for link clicks, the system looks for likely clickers, not necessarily buyers. Use purchase or qualified-lead optimization when tracking and volume support it.

### Match objective to evidence

| Need | Useful setup | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Store sales | website purchase | contribution, new customer |
| Corporate enquiries | qualified lead/form | win rate |
| Workshop messages | message with fast staff | booked seats |
| Event awareness | reach/video test | attendance, lift |
| App | valuable in-app event | retention |

If you get only two purchases a week, the system has little outcome data. You might still optimize for purchases with limits, or choose an earlier event that truly predicts purchase. Don't choose an easy event only because the graph looks active.

## 2. Audience and Automation

Advantage+ features can broaden delivery and combine automation with controls such as location or minimum age. The exact treatment of audience suggestions varies by objective and setup. Read what is a hard control and what is only a suggestion in your own account.

A simple start for Mitti: one broad, serviceable geography, age restrictions where required, buyers excluded if the goal is new customers, and several clearly different creatives.

> ⚠️ Housing, employment, finance, social issues and other special categories have extra targeting and authorization rules. Don't bypass them.

### Audience choices in plain words

| Audience | Use |
|---|---|
| Broad in served area | let creative and conversion find people |
| Interest suggestion | early hypothesis |
| Customer list | exclusion or permitted relationship |
| Website visitors | helpful reminder |
| Video/engagement | next chapter of a story |
| Lookalike/similar where available | test, not magic |

Upload customer lists only with a lawful basis and permission, platform compliance and security. A spreadsheet from a shopkeeper doesn't automatically qualify.

### Overlap

Three ad sets can compete for the same people. With a small budget, combine similar audiences and give the account enough signal.

## 3. Creative Is the Big Lever

Different crop colours aren't different ideas. Vary the customer, problem, proof, format or story.

| Angle | Hook | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Occasion | "Their first home deserves more than a hamper" | gift video |
| Proof | "Can our parcel survive this drop?" | demo |
| Objection | "Never touched clay? Start here" | class clip |
| Identity | "For people who host at home" | table photo |
| Comparison | "Hamper vs something they'll use" | carousel |

### Creative brief

```text
Customer + moment:
Barrier:
Single promise:
Proof:
Opening frame:
Scenes/copy:
Required disclosure:
CTA and landing page:
What result would support this idea?
```

❌ **Weak**
```text
Shop our new collection now!
```

✅ **Strong**
```text
Moving-in gift sorted: two handmade mugs, gift note included.
We show the delivery date before checkout, and replace breakages.
Choose the set → 
```

### Build 5 × 3 creative concepts

| Barrier | Founder | Demonstration | Customer voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Might break | explains policy | drop test | real unboxing |
| Too costly | making process | price breakdown | why it was worth it |
| Wrong size | sizing help | in-hand comparison | daily use |
| Late | dispatch process | packing day | on-time message |
| Impersonal | note story | personalize online | recipient reaction |

Fifteen cells give you genuinely different material. You don't need to film all of them at once: choose five barriers, then the most natural format for each.

### Three complete ads

```text
A. PROOF
[box falls] We tested 40 drops so your gift isn't the test.
See the packing. Dispatch date shown before payment.

B. BEGINNER
[bowl collapses] This is everyone's first bowl.
Clay, tools, firing and patient help. Saturday, max 8 people.

C. COST
₹850 for a mug? Here's the clay, making, failed pieces and packing.
If handmade matters to you, choose one you'll use every day.
```

Compare not only which gets clicks, but which brings profitable customers.

### Creative inspection sheet

- [ ] Different situation, barrier, proof or story
- [ ] First frame works silently
- [ ] Brand natural, not necessarily first
- [ ] Claim and price true
- [ ] No before/after implication or personal attribute that breaks rules
- [ ] Captions, consent, licence and disclosure
- [ ] Matching page

## 4. Budget and Test Design

Suppose break-even CPA is ₹810 and Mitti can afford to learn with ₹16,200. That funds about 20 purchases **at break-even**, not guaranteed purchases.

Before launch, write:

- Hypothesis and main outcome.
- Budget and dates.
- Creative difference.
- Stop-loss for broken tracking, unsafe comments or clear misspend.
- Decision for success, failure and unclear.

> 🔑 "Wait 50 conversions" and "kill after one CPA" are common shortcuts. Neither proves performance. Volume, cost, variation and delay matter. Use platform experiments or geo/holdout tests for larger decisions.

## 5. Retargeting

Show a reason that helps: size guide to product viewers, proof to hesitant shoppers, reminder to cart users. Exclude purchasers where appropriate and cap pressure. If almost everyone would have bought anyway, high retargeting ROAS may contain little added profit.

## 6. Diagnose the Funnel

| Pattern | Likely area | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Expensive reach | auction, narrow audience, creative | geography, season, variety |
| Views, low clicks | message/offer | first frame, relevance, CTA |
| Clicks, few page views | speed/tracking | load and event setup |
| Views, few carts | page/product | match, price, proof |
| Carts, few orders | checkout | fees, payments, delivery |
| Leads, few sales | quality/follow-up | form and response time |
| Frequency up, results down | saturation, possibly | creative, reach and seasonality |

Don't call it fatigue from frequency alone.

## 7. Scaling

Increase spend gradually and watch **marginal CPA**.

```text
Marginal CPA = additional spend / additional orders
```

If spend rises ₹10,000 → ₹15,000 and orders rise 20 → 25, the extra CPA is ₹1,000, although average CPA is ₹600. With ₹810 break-even, the extra spend loses money.

### 💻 Pixel and Conversions API

Send browser and server events with the same event name and `event_id` for deduplication. Confirm consent, currency, value, test/live separation and data minimization. "More events" isn't better if they are duplicates.

> 👔 **Owner's report:** spend, new customers, contribution after ads, creative learning and next decision. Screenshots of reach aren't enough.

## 9. A First Campaign Step by Step

1. Verify business ownership, payment responsibility and account safety.
2. Connect the correct Page, Instagram account, pixel/dataset and domain.
3. Test purchase or qualified lead from start to finish.
4. Choose one objective and conversion.
5. Use a broad but serviceable audience.
6. Create three to five distinct ads.
7. Choose placements you can serve well; check previews.
8. Set budget, dates and stop rules.
9. Publish, test links and spend.
10. Write a learning log, not just results.

### Seven-day review

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | delivery, policy, broken link, comments |
| 2 | spend and tracking reconciliation |
| 3 | no panic edits without a safety/tracking problem |
| 5 | pattern by concept and page |
| 7 | compare with pre-written decision rule |

Conversion delays matter. Yesterday's CPA often improves as later purchases arrive; use your own typical delay.

## 10. Read a Creative Test

| Ad | Spend | Link visits | Carts | Orders | CPA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proof | ₹6,000 | 420 | 30 | 9 | ₹667 |
| Beginner | ₹5,200 | 510 | 18 | 5 | ₹1,040 |
| Cost | ₹4,800 | 250 | 22 | 7 | ₹686 |

Proof and Cost are below the ₹810 break-even but not below Mitti's ₹560 target. Beginner gets cheap visits and weak buying, perhaps because the product is a set rather than a class. Don't declare a universal winner from 9 versus 7 orders. Make a new version of both surviving ideas, and route the Beginner concept to workshop bookings.

### Learning statement

```text
We believe gift buyers respond to packing proof because 9 of 21 observed
orders came from that ad. Confidence is low: delivery wasn't equal, and
orders are few. Next: controlled test of two proof stories, same page.
```

## 11. Comments Are Part of the Ad

Check daily. Answer price, delivery and fit; protect privacy; hide spam according to rules; fix true complaints. Don't delete fair questions about cost.

```text
Why so expensive?
₹850 includes clay, about 40 minutes of making, two firings, failed
pieces and packing. We also have a ₹590 tumbler if you prefer.
```

Publish the numbers only if accurate. A calm answer is sometimes the most persuasive part of the ad.

## 12. Lead Ads Without Wasting Calls

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Use/quantity | qualification |
| Date | urgency |
| Work email | quote |
| Preferred contact | respectful follow-up |

```text
Cost per sale = CPL / lead-to-sale rate
```

At ₹180 CPL and 3% sales, cost is ₹6,000. At ₹500 CPL and 12%, it's ₹4,167. The more expensive lead is cheaper. Contact promptly, make identity clear and feed outcomes back.

## 13. Retargeting Plan

| Recent action | Show | Exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Watched half the drop test | gift guide | buyers |
| Viewed product | sizes, date, care | recent buyers |
| Started checkout | payment/help answer | buyers, refusals as applicable |
| Bought | care or suitable next item, with permission | repeated first-purchase ad |

Use an experiment or holdout where possible. People who abandoned a cart were already close to buying.

## 14. Scaling Without Breaking Profit

| Step | Check |
|---|---|
| Raise modestly | marginal CPA after conversion delay |
| Add concept | new situation, not duplicate |
| Add geography | shipping and margin |
| Add platform | genuine audience and capacity |
| Add stock | cash and lead time |

```text
Day range  Average CPA  Marginal CPA  Decision
1-7        ₹600         -             learn
8-14       ₹650         ₹720          hold/improve
15-21      ₹720         ₹980          step back
```

Average CPA can look acceptable while the last spend is unprofitable.

> 👔 **Agency brief:** we own accounts; contribution is ₹{{x}}; target new-customer CPA is ₹{{y}}; show creative learning and marginal results; no guaranteed ROAS claims.

## 15. Policies and Account Problems

- Read the rejection reason and official policy.
- Fix the whole page and claim, not only one word.
- Keep business and identity information consistent.
- Request review through official tools.
- Don't buy "aged" ad accounts or bypass restrictions.

Attribution windows, targeting options and automation settings change. Record which setting was used with each report, and check Meta's help page before comparing different periods.

## 🛠️ Practical Lab: Build a Safe Meta Ads Test

**You will finish with:** business-owned assets, tested events and a paused creative test. Time: 6 hours. **You can stop before payment.**

### Tools and registration

| Job | Tool | Open |
|---|---|---|
| Business assets | Meta Business Suite/portfolio | [business.facebook.com](https://business.facebook.com/) |
| Ads | Ads Manager | [adsmanager.facebook.com](https://adsmanager.facebook.com/) |
| Events | Events Manager | [business.facebook.com/events_manager](https://business.facebook.com/events_manager/) |
| Public competitor ads | Meta Ad Library | [facebook.com/ads/library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) |
| Training | Meta Blueprint | [facebook.com/business/learn](https://www.facebook.com/business/learn) |

### Secure business setup

1. Use a real personal login with two-factor and a business email. Don't create a fake profile.
2. Business Suite → Settings → create/confirm a business portfolio owned by the client.
3. Add/verify Page and Instagram; don't accidentally claim assets owned by someone else.
4. Accounts → Ad accounts → Add/Create. Choose currency/time zone carefully.
5. Add people with minimum roles; keep two business administrators.
6. Data Sources → Datasets → create/connect one dataset/pixel. Connect it to the correct ad account.
7. Brand Safety/Domains or current equivalent → add website domain and follow available verification.
8. Payment method and spending control belong to authorized business owner.

### Install and test events

1. Make an event sheet: page view, view content, add to cart, lead or purchase. Don't send names/email as event parameters.
2. Use supported integration or GTM; don't install a second pixel if one already exists.
3. In Events Manager → Test events, open site with consent state you expect.
4. Make one product/lead test. Confirm event name, value/currency and URL.
5. If using Conversions API, use the same Pixel/Dataset, matching `event_name` and shared `event_id` for deduplication.
6. Test consent rejection, failed payment, refresh and repeat click.
7. Compare shop/CRM with event total before choosing a conversion goal.

### Make the campaign but leave it paused

1. Ads Manager → Create → choose Sales, Leads or matching objective.
2. Name campaign `MITTI_2026-12_gifts_sales`.
3. Define conversion location and one real event. Check attribution setting.
4. Location, serviceable age restrictions and other required controls. Review Advantage+ suggestions versus hard controls.
5. Budget plan from contribution, not a "minimum you can afford".
6. Choose placements based on creative; preview each.
7. Make three different ads: packing proof, size decision, real gift situation. Use original files, rights and valid UTMs.
8. Preview Facebook and Instagram. Check text crop, destination, comments, disclosure and page load.
9. Save as draft or keep every campaign/ad set/ad **off**.
10. Document exactly what would change and stop before payment if learning.

### Creative worksheet

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | might break | falling box | 10-drop result | gift page | does proof move doubt? |
| B | size unsure | mug in hand | measured guide | size guide | does decision help? |

Three filename copies of the same video aren't three concepts.

### After an authorized small test

Wait for delivery/conversion delay. Compare spend, qualified actions, contribution and comments by concept. Pause for misleading creative, safety, broken tracking or exceeded cap. Don't decide from one click, and don't scale before a marginal-CPA check.

### If it goes wrong

| Problem | Do |
|---|---|
| Asset already claimed | identify owner, request access/correction |
| Pixel missing | check Page URL, integration, consent, ad blocker |
| Event twice | shared event ID and one browser install |
| Ad rejected/restricted | fix policy/identity, request review; don't create evasion accounts |
| Wrong audience | hard controls versus Advantage+ suggestion and location |

## ❓ Common Questions

### How do I start Facebook and Instagram ads?

Verify account ownership, test tracking, choose one real outcome, use a broad but serviceable audience, make three to five distinct ads, set a loss limit and review against a rule written before launch.

### Why are my Meta ads getting clicks but no sales?

Check whether clicks become page views, whether the page matches the ad, price and delivery clarity, checkout errors and lead or order tracking. Cheap clicks may also come from a creative that attracts the wrong people.

### What is marginal CPA?

It is the cost of the extra orders from extra spend. If spend rises ₹5,000 and orders rise by five, marginal CPA is ₹1,000, even if the average still looks acceptable.
