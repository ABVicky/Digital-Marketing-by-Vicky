🔎 Customer Research & Positioning

Module 02 · By Vicky Prasad Mahato · Updated 2026-11-23

🟢 Stage 1 · Foundations · ⏱️ ~3 hours

Meera describes her mugs as "food-safe, wheel-thrown stoneware". A customer tells a friend, "My mother never buys anything nice for herself, so I got her one." Meera talks about clay. Her customer talks about love and guilt. The best copy was already spoken; someone had to listen.

🔑 In short: Customer research means learning what real buyers did, worried about and chose instead, using interviews, reviews, search data and support logs. Positioning then states who you serve, in what situation, why you differ and what proves it.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Run five useful customer interviews.
  • Mine reviews and searches for real language.
  • Write a job-to-be-done and a segment.
  • Create a specific positioning statement.

1. Ask About the Past

People are poor at predicting what they will buy. Ask about what they already did.

❌ Weak question

Would you buy handmade plates if they were affordable?

✅ Strong question

Tell me about the last time you bought a gift for someone's home.
What started the search? What did you consider? What nearly stopped you?

A 20-minute interview script

  1. What was happening in your life when you first looked for this?
  2. What did you try or consider first?
  3. How did you find us and the alternatives?
  4. What made you hesitate?
  5. What finally convinced you?
  6. What changed after you bought?
  7. How would you describe us to a friend?
  8. Is there something I should have asked?

Stay quiet after a question. Follow with "Can you tell me more?" Record only with permission, and store notes safely.

Recruit the right people

PersonWhat they teach
Bought in the last 60 daystriggers and final reason
Bought from a rivalwhat you lacked
Started and leftfriction and fear
Repeat buyerlasting value
Never considered youalternatives and awareness

Offer thanks that doesn't depend on praise, such as a small voucher for anyone who takes part. Tell them it's fine to stop or skip a question.

A real-sounding exchange

You:  What was happening when you first looked?
Buyer: My cousin was moving on Sunday. It was Thursday.
You:  What did you try first?
Buyer: A hamper. It felt like something an office sends.
You:  Can you tell me more about "an office sends"?
Buyer: Like I didn't think about her. She'd know.
You:  What nearly stopped you ordering from us?
Buyer: Your site said "dispatched soon". Soon is not Sunday.

One follow-up question found the emotional job ("she'd know I didn't think") and a practical bug ("dispatched soon"). The second is fixable this afternoon.

Mistakes interviewers make

  • Explaining the product halfway through.
  • Filling silence.
  • Asking two questions at once.
  • Arguing with a complaint.
  • Hearing only what supports the plan.

Ask someone else to read two transcripts and list themes before seeing yours.

2. Four Research Sources

SourceWhat to collect
Interviewstriggers, worries, decision process
Reviews (yours and rivals')exact praise and complaints
Search dataquestions and comparisons
Sales/support logsobjections and confusion

Talk to recent buyers, people who left, and people who chose a competitor. Only interviewing fans gives you a flattering picture.

⚠️ Five interviews can reveal problems and language. They cannot prove that "80% of the market" feels a certain way. Use surveys or behavioural data for size.

Surveys: use after interviews

Interviews find the options; surveys estimate how common they are.

❌ Weak survey question

How much do you love our beautiful packaging? (1-10)

✅ Strong survey question

Which ONE thing almost stopped you from ordering?
[ ] Price  [ ] Delivery date  [ ] Fear of breakage
[ ] Not sure about size  [ ] Payment  [ ] Nothing  [ ] Other: ____

Randomize option order where possible, include "other", and report who answered. A survey of existing buyers can't tell you why strangers didn't buy.

4. Jobs to Be Done

When {{situation}}, I want to {{progress}}, so I can {{outcome}}.

For Mitti: When my friend moves into a home and I'm short on time, I want a gift that feels personal, so I can show I put thought into it without spending days searching.

Useful segments share a situation and need. "Women aged 25–34" contains many unrelated jobs.

5. Research Competitors

List direct (other ceramic brands), indirect (hampers) and do nothing (a bank transfer). For each, record promise, price, proof, channel, strength and complaint. Study ads in public libraries, but don't assume a long-running ad is profitable.

6. Positioning

Positioning is the context that makes your value obvious.

For {{specific customer}} who {{situation}},
{{brand}} is a {{familiar category}} that {{main value}}.
Unlike {{real alternative}}, we {{meaningful difference}}.
You can believe it because {{proof}}.

❌ Weak

Premium quality handmade products for everyone.

✅ Strong

For people giving a housewarming gift, Mitti Studio makes everyday
ceramics that feel personal without weeks of planning. Unlike generic
hampers, each set is made in our Kolkata studio and includes the
maker's note. Every order is drop-tested packed and replaced if broken.

Only use those claims if they are true. If Mitti hasn't drop-tested its packaging, it must not say so.

💡 The swap test: put a competitor's name in your statement. If it still works, be more specific.

👔 Owner's choice: positioning means deciding who you are not optimizing for. Serving everyone usually means speaking to no one.

💻 Scale it carefully: export permitted review data, remove names and contact details, cluster themes, then have a human read samples from every cluster. Check each platform's terms before collecting data.

7. Build a One-Page Customer Profile

Situation: buying a housewarming gift within 10 days
Trigger: invitation or moving date
Job: show I thought about them without weeks of searching
Alternatives: hamper, plant, cash, large retailer
Push: generic gifts feel careless
Pull: handmade, personal note
Anxiety: late, broken, wrong size
Habit: ordering the same hamper every time
Proof that helps: date, drop test, photos in hand
Where they look: friends, search, Instagram, marketplaces
Words they use: "thoughtful", "she'd use it", "will it reach by"
Evidence: interviews 1-8, survey Nov, support log
Last reviewed:

No favourite colour, pet name or stock photo. Keep what changes a decision.

8. Estimate Whether the Segment Is Big Enough

You don't need a perfect market size. You need to know if the segment can support the goal.

Monthly buyers needed = goal orders from this segment
Reachable people = those we can find through known channels
Required share = buyers needed / reachable people per month

Suppose Mitti needs 60 gift orders a month. Keyword tools, partner data and social reach suggest 6,000 reachable people with a relevant occasion each month. It needs 1%. If only 300 were reachable, it would need 20%, which is a warning to widen the situation, add channels or lower the goal.

Show where each number comes from and give a range. A single precise number from a vague source is decoration.

9. Turn Positioning Into Messages

Objection answerbreakage replacedcheckout
Actionchoose a ready-to-gift setbuttons

Test the message before building

  1. Show five relevant strangers the headline and first paragraph.
  2. Ask: "What is sold? For whom? What would you do next? What worries you?"
  3. Note their words exactly.
  4. Rewrite and repeat with five more.

If two of five misunderstand the category, don't buy traffic yet.

🛠️ Practical Lab: Run Five Interviews and Mine 50 Reviews

You will finish with: real quotes, three themes and a tested positioning line. Time: 6–8 hours over a week.

Tools

NotesGoogle Sheetssheets.google.com
Search languageGoogle autocomplete, AnswerThePublicanswerthepublic.com
Conversationsrelevant Reddit, Quora or Facebook groupsfollow each community's rules

Where to find people

  1. Recent customers from your own records, contacted with a proper basis.
  2. People in your building, class, office or hobby group.
  3. Customers at a partner shop, with the owner's permission.
  4. A community post that says clearly you want to learn, not sell.

Step by step

  1. Make a three-question screener: Did you buy or look for {{category}} in the last 90 days? What did you choose? Can we speak for 20 minutes?
  2. Choose two recent buyers, one who left, one who chose a rival and one who has never heard of you.
  3. Send the Appendix B interview invitation. Offer the same thank-you to everyone.
  4. Ask permission before recording. If they say no, take notes.
  5. Use the eight-question script. Speak less than a quarter of the time.
  6. Within an hour, add 5–10 exact quotes to the sheet: Person ID, Quote, Type, Situation. Use P1, P2, not names.
  7. Mine 50 reviews: read your own and competitors' reviews on pages you may access. Copy short anonymous phrases by hand into Trigger, Worry, Praise, Complaint. Don't scrape.
  8. Sort by theme. Count people, not repeated quotes.
  9. Write three positioning lines. Show them to five other people for five seconds each.

Practice task for Mitti

Interview three people who bought a gift recently. Find their trigger, their rejected alternative and what nearly stopped them. Then rewrite Mitti's first headline using their words.

🏋️ Exercises

Exercise 2.1: Fix three questions

Rewrite: (a) "Do you like our site?" (b) "Would you recommend us?" (c) "How much would you pay?"

View Solution

(a) "Show me how you'd find a gift under ₹2,000; think aloud." (b) "Have you told anyone about us? What did you say?" (c) "What did you pay last time, and what else did you compare?"

Exercise 2.2: Position your business

Write three versions, each with a different alternative. Show five target customers and ask, "What is this, who is it for, and why choose it?"

View Success Check

Most should explain it without help. Where their words differ from yours, rewrite using theirs.

Exercise 2.3: Find the job

"I bought the class because my daughter is always on her phone. She put it down for two hours. The bowl is terrible but she won't let me throw it away." Identify push, pull, outcome and a message.

View Solution

Push: constant phone use. Pull: hands-on shared activity. Outcome: undistracted time and a keepsake. Message: "Two hours together, and something they'll want to keep." Avoid shaming parents, and don't claim a lasting behaviour change.

Exercise 2.4: Segment check

You need 30 sales a month from a segment with about 900 reachable people. What share is required, and what would you do?

View Solution

30 ÷ 900 = 3.3% each month. That may be ambitious. Check repeat purchases, additional channels, adjacent situations and whether 900 refreshes monthly.

❓ Common Questions

How many customer interviews do I need?

Five to eight per segment usually reveal repeated triggers, worries and language. They can't tell you what percentage of a market feels that way. Use a survey or behavioural data when you need to estimate size.

What questions should I ask in a customer interview?

Ask about the last real purchase: what started the search, what else they considered, what nearly stopped them, what convinced them and how they'd describe it to a friend. Avoid "Would you buy this?" because people predict themselves poorly.

What is a positioning statement?

It is a short internal statement of customer, situation, category, main value, real alternative, difference and proof. If a competitor's name fits into it just as well, it isn't specific enough yet.

✅ Quick Quiz

  1. Best evidence of demand?
    • A) "I love the idea"
    • B) A recent attempt to solve the problem
    • C) A like
  2. What is wrong with the segment "everyone who drinks tea"?
    • A) Too broad to share one buying situation
    • B) It includes coffee drinkers
    • C) Nothing
  3. What should be counted when finding themes?
    • A) Mentions only
    • B) People and context
    • C) Longest quotes
  4. When are surveys most useful?
    • A) Before any interview
    • B) After interviews reveal likely options
    • C) Never
View Answers
  1. B.
  2. A.
  3. B. One person may repeat a point many times.
  4. B. Otherwise you may offer the wrong choices.
