💼 Module 15: LinkedIn Marketing

🟠 Stage 3 · Social, Email & Ads · ⏱️ ~4 hours

Mitti's corporate gifting posts said, "Elevate your corporate gifting experience." Nobody replied. Then Meera wrote: "We quoted 300 mugs last week and lost the order. The HR manager told me why: our invoice had no purchase-order field. Fixed today. What else slows down small vendors?" Forty-one comments. Three enquiries.

Professionals respond to specific experience. They scroll past slogans.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Decide between personal profile and company page.
  • Write posts from real work.
  • Build a small account list and start respectful conversations.
  • Understand LinkedIn Ads and when their cost makes sense.

1. When LinkedIn Fits

FitQuestion
Buyer has a work roleCan you name the role and company type?
Value is high enoughWill one customer repay slow, costly work?
Expertise mattersDo you have real cases or opinions?
Hiring/partnershipDo you need talent or partners?

Selling ₹400 mugs one at a time? Probably not. Selling 300 at a time to HR teams? Worth testing.

People usually prefer to talk to people. A company page still matters when a buyer checks whether you're real.

Profile as a landing page

❌ Weak headline

Founder | Visionary | Disruptor | Dreamer

✅ Strong headline

I help HR teams send handmade gifts people keep | Founder, Mitti Studio
  • Photo: clear, recent.
  • Banner: one sentence and proof.
  • About: who, problem, approach, evidence, next step.
  • Featured: gifting page, sample, case.
  • Experience: outcomes and role, with permission.

Company page checklist

  • [ ] Plain one-line description
  • [ ] Website, industry, size, location
  • [ ] Button to the right page
  • [ ] Two administrators
  • [ ] Three recent useful posts

3. Post From Work

Keep a work log. Each day note one mistake, question, choice, number or disagreement.

TypeMitti example
Lessonwhy we lost the 300-mug order
Breakdowntrue cost of a custom gift
Checklist9 questions before ordering gifts
Contrarian"Don't put a big logo on it"
Processhow a sample is approved
Proofbefore/after packaging test
Questionwhat makes vendors easy to work with?

Structure

Line 1-2: specific tension or result
Context: just enough
What happened / steps
What we learned, including limits
One question or next step

❌ Weak

I am humbled and thrilled to announce that we are excited to leverage
synergies in the gifting space. Agree?

✅ Strong

"Can you deliver 200 by the 12th?" We said yes too fast.

Two glazes failed. We delivered 140 and paid for a courier for the rest.

Now every bulk quote has three dates: sample, firing buffer, dispatch.
It looks less impressive, and HR managers thank us for it.

If you order custom gifts, ask vendors: "What's your failure buffer?"

Avoid inventing emotional stories. People in your field notice, and colleagues will know.

Document posts

Turn a useful checklist into 6–10 readable pages. One idea per page, large text, a final page with a next step. People save and forward checklists to teammates.

4. Comments Are Content

Ten thoughtful comments a week on posts your buyers read can beat five posts of your own.

Add: a detail, counterexample, number or useful question.
Don't: "Great post!", pitch, or copy AI text into every thread.

5. Outreach Without Being "That Person"

Build a list of 30

FieldExample
Company200–800 employees, Kolkata
Triggerhiring many people, anniversary, new office
PersonHR/people operations, procurement
Relevanceonboarding gifts
Sourcepublic role, event, mutual contact
Next step/datecomment, then note

Use information lawfully and within platform rules. Automated scraping and mass-connection tools can break terms and damage accounts.

Connection note

❌ Weak

Hi, I want to connect and explore synergies. Are you free for a call?

✅ Strong

Hi {{name}}, your post about first-week onboarding was useful, especially
the desk-kit idea. We make ceramic desk sets in Kolkata, and I'm
collecting what HR teams need from small vendors. Happy to connect.

Message ladder

  1. Useful connection, no pitch.
  2. Answer or resource tied to their situation.
  3. A specific, easy question.
  4. Offer a sample or short call.
  5. Stop after a reasonable follow-up.
You mentioned 60 joiners in January. I made a one-page checklist of lead
times and invoice details HR teams asked us for. Want it? No call needed.

7. Measure

LevelMetric
Right audienceshare of viewers/commenters in target roles
Conversationuseful comments, replies
Intentprofile and page visits, checklist requests
Pipelinequalified meetings, samples, quotes, won value
Pipeline per hour = qualified pipeline value / hours spent

A post with 900 views and two HR managers asking for samples beats one with 60,000 views from students, if you sell corporate gifts.

8. LinkedIn Ads

LinkedIn can target by professional attributes, and usually costs more per click than many social networks. Third-party sources report a low daily minimum, but a practical test needs more. Confirm minimums in Campaign Manager.

ObjectiveAsset
Awareness in a roledocument, video
Considerationcase, checklist, employee post promoted with permission
Leadslead-gen form or page
Retargetingproof for visitors, where permitted

Do the maths first

Mitti's average corporate order: 150 sets × ₹1,300 = ₹1,95,000. Contribution 30% = ₹58,500. Suppose:

Cost per lead            ₹2,500
Lead -> qualified        40%
Qualified -> won         15%
Cost per won order = 2500 / (0.40 x 0.15) = ₹41,667

₹41,667 is below ₹58,500, leaving ₹16,833 before sales time and overheads. At a 10% win rate, cost becomes ₹62,500 and it loses money. The rate you can't control yet decides the result, so test small.

Lead form or landing page?

Lead formLanding page
easy, prefilledmore explanation
more volumeoften clearer intent
needs fast follow-upneeds good mobile page

Ask one qualifying question. Connect the form to the CRM (Module 23).

👔 45 minutes a day for six weeks: 10 min comments, 15 min writing, 10 min list, 10 min replies.

💻 Tech: pass lead ID, campaign and consent to CRM; import qualified and won stages if available; deduplicate by lead ID; restrict who sees contact data.

9. For Job Seekers and Freelancers

  • Headline: problem you solve + proof.
  • Featured: two honest case studies (Module 27).
  • Weekly post: one lesson from practice work, labelled.
  • Outreach: ask for feedback on a specific work sample, not "a job please".

🏋️ Exercises

Exercise 15.1: Rewrite

"We are a leading provider of end-to-end digital solutions."

View Sample

"We fix slow checkout pages for online stores that sell in India. Usual work: two weeks, a before/after report, and a handover your team can follow." Use only facts you can support.

Exercise 15.2: Ad maths

Cost per lead ₹1,800, 30% qualified, 20% of qualified won, contribution ₹35,000. Profitable before sales time?

View Solution

1,800 ÷ (0.30 × 0.20) = ₹30,000 per won order. ₹5,000 remains, so it's marginal. Sales hours may erase it.

Exercise 15.3: Work log

Keep five days of notes and publish the two most useful.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Real work beats slogans.
  • Comments and conversations are part of the plan.
  • B2B buying involves several people.
  • Calculate ad economics from won deals.

✅ Quick Quiz

  1. Best LinkedIn metric for B2B sales?
    • A) Likes
    • B) Qualified conversations and won value
    • C) Connections
  2. A first message should:
    • A) Pitch immediately
    • B) Be specific and useful
    • C) Include a price list
  3. What can make a low cost per lead unprofitable?
    • A) Low qualification and win rates
    • B) A blue logo
    • C) Long posts
View Answers
  1. B.
  2. B.
  3. A. Cost per won customer is what matters.
