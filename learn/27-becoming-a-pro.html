🌟 Becoming a Pro

Module 27 · By Vicky Prasad Mahato · Updated 2026-11-23

🌟 Stage 5 · Pro · ⏱️ ~4 hours

A beginner says, "I can run ads." A professional asks what business decision the ads should support, what could go wrong and how everyone will know whether they worked.

🔑 In short: To become a digital marketer, learn the whole funnel, go deep in one specialty, and build two or three honest case studies that show evidence, decisions, results and limits. Clear thinking and reliable work matter more than certificates.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Choose a T-shaped skill path.
  • Build honest portfolio evidence.
  • Handle proposals, reports and interviews.
  • Keep learning after this guide.

2. Portfolio Without Clients

Use your own site, a volunteer organization with written permission, or the fictional Mitti client. Label fictional work clearly. Don't present a mock dashboard as a real result.

Case-study format

Context and constraint:
My role:
Evidence and diagnosis:
Decision and alternatives:
What I made:
Result with dates and comparison:
What I can't claim:
What I learned and would do next:

❌ Weak

Increased sales by 300% using SEO.

✅ Strong

Over 60 days, bookings rose from 12 to 18 after a page redesign. Ads and
season also changed, so this doesn't prove the page caused all 6. User
tests fell from 4 of 5 people missing price to 0 of 5.

This is an example format, not a real claim.

A truthful case-study example

Context: A three-person class studio with a 12-seat Saturday session.
Role: I interviewed 8 beginners, rewrote the page and redesigned the
reminder flow. The teacher delivered the class.
Evidence: 5 of 8 feared being the only beginner; 3 missed pickup terms.
Action: "Everyone starts here", clear pickup date, two consented reminders.
Observed: kept bookings 9 → 11 across two comparable Saturdays.
Limit: one instructor also taught an extra demo; not randomized.
Learning: fear and pickup information deserve their own section.
Next: three more weeks, ask a missed-booking reason, then assess.

This is fictional training evidence, labelled as such. A portfolio should show decisions, trade-offs and what you learned, not simply a rising line chart.

Portfolio site structure

Home: one promise + three relevant projects
Project page: problem → evidence → decision → artifact → result → limits
About: skills, roles, location/time zone, collaboration style
Contact: one clear method
Resume: verified dates and skills
Writing: useful answers from real practice

Redact client information, get permission, and don't publish confidential screenshots.

3. Client Discovery

  1. What business result matters, by when?
  2. Who decides and who is affected?
  3. What data and permissions exist?
  4. What was tried?
  5. What are margin, capacity and cash limits?
  6. What happens to leads?
  7. What must not happen?

Red flags: guaranteed ranking, no access to facts, unlawful data, undefined success, refusal to pay for work.

A client discovery example

❌ Weak

How many reels do you want? We guarantee 10 lakh reach.

✅ Strong

You said bookings are down. Which booking type, in what dates, from
what baseline? How many enquiries were answered? Which capacity and
margin constraint matters? What customer evidence do you have? What
would change if the next test fails?

Useful discovery separates a problem from a requested deliverable. A client may ask for Instagram posts when missed calls are the real leak.

Keep service fee and ad spend separate. The client should normally own business accounts, data and domains. Use role-based access instead of shared passwords.

Proposal case: a 30-day local test

ItemAgreement
Understandinganswer whether a beginner page + reply process raises kept bookings
Scope8 interviews, page copy, 3 posts, search test plan, reporting
Not includedphotography, guaranteed rankings, client operations
Client givesaccurate stock, teacher time, website and approved access

Show fee, tax/invoice terms, payment date, deliverables and cancellation clearly. Use a contract appropriate to your country and work.

Negotiating scope

If a client needs a lower price, remove or delay deliverables instead of silently doing the same work for less. If results depend on the client changing stock or response time, name that dependency in the proposal.

5. Price Your Work

Hourly floor = (living + business + tax reserve + profit) / realistic billable hours

Compare with market and value. Use a small paid discovery when scope is uncertain. Don't link all pay to sales if you can't control product, stock and follow-up.

6. Monthly Report

  1. Executive decision in three sentences.
  2. Goal, driver, guardrails.
  3. What changed and evidence.
  4. Work completed.
  5. Failures and limits.
  6. Next priorities, owner and budget.

Bad news early with a plan earns more trust than a pretty chart late.

Report makeover

❌ Weak

Reach: 45K. Likes: 1.1K. Great month!

✅ Strong

Goal: 20 confirmed beginner bookings, contribution positive.
Result: 13 kept bookings; capacity was 24.
What we learned: beginners asked whether they could restart after a
mistake. Our page didn't say. We can't prove the page caused 13.
Next: answer this fear on page, test two opening stories with a ₹6,000
cap, and report kept bookings plus cancellation reasons on 15 Dec.

The report makes a next decision possible, even if the first result wasn't the target.

8. Career Paths

  • Specialist: SEO, media, lifecycle, CRO, analytics.
  • Generalist: own a small business's growth.
  • Freelancer/agency: delivery plus sales and client management.
  • Founder: customer and product judgement.
  • Leader: strategy, people, budget and learning system.

Read job descriptions from several employers and save a dated skills matrix. Titles vary, so match responsibilities.

9. Keep Current

Weekly: one official product update, one customer conversation and one data question. Monthly: one documented experiment. Quarterly: retire outdated tactics.

👔 Build business judgement: pricing, operations, finance and communication.

💻 Build reliability: data models, tests, access, documentation and monitoring.

11. Resume and Interview Examples

❌ Weak bullet

Managed social media and increased engagement by 400%.

✅ Stronger bullet format

Mapped customer questions and redesigned a gift-size guide; 5 of 5 user
tests then found the delivery date, compared with 1 of 5 before. Built
and documented the content workflow. [Use only if this was your work.]

Interview case

"A client wants to double ad spend because ROAS is 4."

Say: Verify revenue, refunds and margin. Segment brand, retargeting and new customers. Check capacity, incrementality and marginal cost. Test a small increment. Decide based on contribution and limits. Don't promise the doubled ROAS.

Questions to ask an employer

  • What does a useful campaign result look like here?
  • What customer research can I hear this week?
  • How are data access and decisions protected?
  • Which channel has the clearest current constraint?
  • How does the team learn from failed work?

12. Work in a Team

PersonAgreement
Strategist + analystmetric and decision definitions
Copy + designone message and proof
Marketing + productstock, promises and support
Developer + marketerevent and privacy contract
Founder + freelancerbudget, approval and ownership

Write a one-page responsibility matrix: responsible does it, accountable approves it, consulted gives input, informed receives result. One named accountable person is enough for each decision.

Asking for feedback

"Does this look good?" gives politeness. Ask: "What do you think this sells, what would stop you, and what proof is missing?" Thank people and change only with a reason.

You will finish with: a live portfolio, two certificates if useful, ten applications or pitches and a proposal. Time: two weeks part-time.

A certificate shows you studied. A case study shows you can think. Do both, in a 1:3 time ratio.

Build a portfolio, step by step

  1. Choose a place: GitHub Pages, WordPress, Notion public page or a builder.
  2. Pages: Home, three projects, About, Contact.
  3. Home line: I help {{who}} {{do what}} using {{specialty}}.
  4. Project 1: your Module 05 page test. Project 2: Module 20/21 tracking. Project 3: your specialty.
  5. For each: problem → evidence → decision → artifact → result → limit.
  6. Blur private data; label practice work; get written permission for real clients.
  7. Add the link to LinkedIn Featured, GitHub and resume.
  8. Ask three people: What do I do? What is missing? Fix it.

⚠️ Avoid "jobs" that ask for deposits, your bank login, fake reviews or bulk account creation.

Send ten good applications

  1. Build a table: role, company, proof they want, my proof, gap, sent, reply, next.
  2. Change the top three resume bullets for each.
  3. Send a 90-word note: what you noticed, your relevant proof, one question.
  4. Follow up once after a week.

Freelance starter offer

Service: Landing-page clarity check
You get: 5 user tests, 1-page findings, 3 ranked fixes, 20-min call
Not included: design, ads, guaranteed sales
Needs: page link, goal, permission to test
Time: 5 working days   Fee: {{amount}}   50% to begin

Ask your accountant about invoices, GST and tax. Use a written agreement, take client access by role, not password, and remove it at the end.

Mock interview

Record yourself answering, in two minutes each:

  1. Sales fell 30% this week. What do you check?
  2. ROAS is 4. Should we double spend?
  3. Show a project that failed.
  4. How would you spend ₹25,000 for a tutor?
  5. Why do GA4 and Meta disagree?

Use Situation → Decision → Evidence → Result → Limit. Watch once, improve one thing, record again.

🏋️ Exercises

Exercise 27.1

Create one honest case study with dates, role, evidence and limits.

Exercise 27.2

Answer: "Why did orders double?" with five alternative explanations besides your campaign.

View Examples

Season, stock availability, competitor outage, price change, tracking change, press mention or another channel.

Exercise 27.3: Your portfolio

Write a case study for work you did in this guide. Label fictional parts, include method, limits and a next decision.

View Success Check

Someone can see the problem, evidence, your exact role, artifact, limits and next step. No client secrets or invented business result appear.

Exercise 27.4: Present the trade-off

Explain in 90 seconds why you would stop a campaign with high likes but negative contribution.

View Sample Structure

Goal and actual contribution → evidence of reach without profitable action → alternative explanations checked → stop or bounded next test → learning kept. Avoid blaming creative without the numbers.

❓ Common Questions

How do I build a digital marketing portfolio with no clients?

Use your own project, permitted volunteer work or a clearly labelled fictional brand. Show the problem, evidence, what you made, the result and what you can't claim. Never present invented results as real.

Which digital marketing skill should I learn first?

Customer research, copy and unit economics help in every role. Then try a week each of SEO, paid ads, content, lifecycle, analytics and conversion work, and choose the one that holds your attention.

Who should own a client's ad accounts?

The client should normally own ad accounts, data and domains, and give the agency or freelancer limited access. Avoid shared passwords and remove access when the work ends.
